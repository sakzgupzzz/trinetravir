# Immunophenotyping of COVID-19 and influenza highlights the role of type I interferons in development of severe COVID-19
**Citation**: Lee JS, Park S, Jeong HW et al. 2020, *Science Immunology* 5(49):eabd1554
**DOI**: 10.1126/sciimmunol.abd1554
**URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7402635/ (PDF downloaded via EuropePMC)
**Status**: downloaded
**Tier**: 1

## Core claim
Lee et al. compare PBMC scRNA-seq profiles between COVID-19 patients (mild + severe + 1 asymptomatic), severe influenza patients, and healthy donors to dissect the host transcriptional response that distinguishes mild from severe SARS-CoV-2 infection. They report that COVID-19 (vs influenza) is dominated by a TNF/IL-1β-driven inflammatory signature across all PBMC cell types, while severe influenza is dominated by interferon-stimulated genes. Critically, in classical monocytes from *severe* COVID-19 (but not mild) they observe coexistence of the COVID-typical TNF/IL-1β signature with an IFN-I/ISG signature — and similar IFN-I-driven inflammation appears in severe influenza. They argue that misplaced/late type I IFN responses are pivotal in driving severe COVID-19 progression and are a candidate therapeutic target.

## Methodology / approach
- **Cohort**: 4 healthy donors + 5 severe influenza + 8 COVID-19 patients (3 sampled at 2 time points, so 11 COVID samples). Asymptomatic case (C8) excluded from main analysis (no replicate). Total ~20 samples.
- **Recruitment**: 3 Korean tertiary hospitals — Asan Medical Center (Seoul), Severance Hospital / Yonsei (Seoul), Chungbuk National University Hospital (Cheongju). Influenza patients enrolled Dec 2015–Apr 2016 (pre-COVID); COVID-19 enrolled in 2020.
- **Severity classification**: National Early Warning Score (NEWS) on day of blood draw — mild = NEWS < 5, severe = NEWS ≥ 5. NEWS components: respiratory rate, SpO2, supplemental O2, temperature, systolic BP, heart rate, consciousness. Influenza severity defined by hospitalization requirement (no NEWS gating).
- **Distribution**: 4 mild COVID samples (16,742 cells), 6 severe COVID samples, 1 asymptomatic, 5 severe flu, 4 HD.
- **Diagnosis**: SARS-CoV-2 confirmed by Allplex 2019-nCoV multiplex RT-PCR (N, RdRP, E genes); Ct values matched between mild and severe groups (no statistically significant difference); flu by rapid antigen test on nasopharyngeal swab.
- **Protocol**: PBMCs isolated by Ficoll-Paque density gradient, **frozen** in liquid nitrogen, thawed on day of library prep (~90% post-thaw viability). Libraries built with **10x Genomics Chromium Single Cell 3′ Library & Gel Bead Kit v3** ("v3" = 3′ chemistry v3, NOT v3.1). Sequencing depth ~50,000 reads/cell on Illumina NextSeq 550 or NovaSeq 6000.
- **Pipeline**: cellranger v3.0.2 mkfastq + count + aggr (default params) → human GRCh38 reference (10x cellranger reference v3.0.0); Seurat v3.1.5; CCA-based sample alignment (top 15 canonical correlation vectors); 15 PCs for tSNE; clustering at resolution 0.5.
- **QC**: cells with <200 genes excluded; cells with >15% mito reads excluded; doublets concentrated in "Uncategorized 1" cluster excluded; total 59,572 cells; mean ~6,900 UMIs and ~1,900 genes per cell.
- **Annotation**: marker genes via Seurat Wilcoxon (logFC>0.25, Bonferroni p<0.05); 22 clusters → 11 immune cell types (B IgG±, EM/non-EM CD4/CD8 T, NK, classical/intermediate/non-classical monocyte, DC) + RBC + platelets + 2 uncategorized.

## Key findings
- COVID-19 PBMCs show a hyper-inflammatory transcriptional signature **across all cell types**, dominated by TNF/IL-1β-driven genes; influenza is dominated by ISGs and antigen-presentation/HLA-class-II genes.
- COVID-19 patients have significantly **lower lymphocyte counts** and **higher CRP** than mild on day of sampling (panel-level confirmation of severity stratification).
- In severe COVID-19 only, classical monocytes co-express the TNF/IL-1β signature **with** an IFN-I/ISG signature; mild COVID-19 lacks this co-expression.
- NFKB1, NFKB2, IRF1, CXCR3 specifically up-regulated in COVID-19; CXCL10, STAT1, TLR4, HLA class II + immunoproteasome subunits specifically up-regulated in flu.
- Severe COVID-19 has elevated classical monocyte fraction; depleted DCs, non-classical/intermediate monocytes, NK cells, EM CD4/CD8 T cells.
- Cell composition shifts validated by flow cytometry on matched samples (Spearman correlation reported).
- WGCNA modules separate disease-specific gene programs at cell-type resolution: G3 (IL1B/NFKBID/OSM in monocytes+DCs in COVID), G2 (GBP1/STAT1/IFITM3/OAS1/IRF3 in T+NK in flu).
- Direct comparison with Wilk 2020 noted by authors: Wilk used Seq-Well; Lee used 10x — and they note this as one explanation for some result divergence.

## Relevance to Trinetravir
**Most useful Tier-1 study for Trinetravir**: 10x Chromium 3′ **v3 chemistry** (compatible with Schulte-Schrepping's 3′ v3.1, and with most cellxgene PBMC studies — small batch effect expected). Severity stratification uses **NEWS**, which maps cleanly onto WHO ordinal scale used by Schulte-Schrepping (mild NEWS<5 ≈ WHO 3-4; severe NEWS≥5 ≈ WHO 5-7) and is recoverable from clinical metadata even if not in cellxgene. Includes a built-in non-COVID viral comparator (severe influenza, n=5) — directly useful for cross-virus generalization tests, but pre-pandemic flu enrollment (Dec 2015 - Apr 2016) introduces a 4-year batch separation from the COVID cohort that we must condition on. Healthy controls are matched within-study (n=4). Time-from-symptom-onset distribution is in their Table S2/S3 (we'll need to retrieve from supplementary if cellxgene metadata is incomplete) and includes longitudinal sampling for 3 patients.

## Caveats / limitations
- Authors' stated: small cohort (n=4 HD + 8 COVID + 5 flu), single-country (Korea), platform difference vs Wilk acknowledged.
- For us additionally: pre-pandemic flu controls means flu patients are confounded with year/season — must include "year of collection" as a covariate or restrict to within-COVID comparisons. Asymptomatic case excluded from analysis but may be present in cellxgene. Asan + Severance + Chungbuk is a 3-site study but they don't break down site-level variance (we may need to add site as a batch covariate). Frozen→thawed PBMCs — cell composition will differ subtly from fresh PBMCs (Wilk uses fresh).
