# A single-cell atlas of the peripheral immune response in patients with severe COVID-19
**Citation**: Wilk AJ, Rustagi A, Zhao NQ et al. 2020, *Nature Medicine* 26(7):1070-1076
**DOI**: 10.1038/s41591-020-0944-y
**URL**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7382903/ (Nature Med). PDF used: medRxiv preprint v1 (2020.04.17.20069930) at https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.04.17.20069930v1.full.pdf — published Nature Medicine version is paywalled and PMC enforces a proof-of-work challenge for PDF download; preprint content is materially identical for the cohort/methods facts we need.
**Status**: downloaded (preprint version)
**Tier**: 1

## Core claim
Wilk et al. apply scRNA-seq to PBMCs from 7 hospitalized COVID-19 patients (8 samples; one patient sampled twice) and 6 healthy controls to build the first single-cell atlas of the peripheral immune response in severe SARS-CoV-2 infection. They report substantial peripheral immune remodeling — heterogeneous interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) signatures, HLA class II downregulation in monocytes, expansion of plasmablasts, and (in ARDS patients only) appearance of a developing/granulocyte-like population — but find that circulating leukocytes do **not** express high levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, arguing against a "cytokine storm" originating in peripheral blood. The work establishes that severity-associated changes in COVID-19 are detectable at the single-cell level in PBMCs and motivates many follow-up cohorts.

## Methodology / approach
- **Cohort**: 7 COVID-19 patients (Stanford ICU Biobank, March-April 2020) + 6 healthy controls (Stanford Profiling Healthy Immunity study). All COVID-19 patients male; ages spanning 20–>80 (banded for de-identification). 8 PBMC samples total (patient C1 sampled twice). Sample collection 2–16 days post symptom onset. Inclusion: adults admitted with RT-PCR-confirmed SARS-CoV-2; co-enrolled in COVID treatment trials; 5 of 7 received remdesivir.
- **Severity classification**: ARDS phenotyped using Berlin criteria (PaO2/FIO2 < 300 on ≥5 cm PEEP + bilateral infiltrates). 4/8 COVID samples were from ventilated ARDS patients; 4/8 from non-ventilated patients. Admission level recorded as ICU vs floor.
- **Healthy controls**: collected separately (different IRB protocol, #26571 vs #28205) — not contemporaneous with COVID-19 cohort. Demographics in their Extended Data Table 1 (not in main text grabbed).
- **Protocol**: **Seq-Well S^3** scRNA-seq platform — *not* 10x Chromium. 15,000 PBMCs loaded per array; mRNA capture beads (ChemGenes); Maxima H-RT for reverse transcription; Nextera XT library prep; sequenced on NovaSeq S2 (CZ Biohub). Read 1 = 12bp cell barcode + 8bp UMI; read 2 = 50bp mRNA.
- **Alignment**: STAR + dropEst against GRCh37 (hg19) augmented with 10 California SARS-CoV-2 strains; no SARS-CoV-2 reads recovered.
- **QC**: cells with <1,000 or >15,000 UMIs filtered; >20% mito or rRNA filtered; >75 genes/100 UMIs filtered as putative multiplets.
- **Total**: 44,271 cells; mean 3,194/sample.
- **Annotation**: Seurat SCTransform → PCA (50 PCs) → UMAP + Louvain; cluster identity by Wilcoxon DE markers, validated against SingleR reference annotation.

## Key findings
- Substantial reconfiguration of peripheral immune-cell composition in COVID-19 vs healthy: depletion of γδ T cells, pDCs, conventional DCs (more pronounced in ARDS).
- Plasmablast expansion in all COVID-19 patients, further elevated in ARDS — without convergent V-gene usage (so not antigen-driven monoclonal expansion in this cohort).
- Heterogeneous ISG signature across donors; ISG response correlates with patient age and inversely with time-from-symptom-onset (consistent with early-transient IFN dynamics).
- HLA class II (HLA-DRA, HLA-DRB1) downregulation in monocytes across all COVID-19 patients, more pronounced in ventilated patients.
- Monocytes and lymphocytes express **low** levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF, IL-1B) — suggests circulating PBMCs are not the source of cytokine storm.
- Novel "developing neutrophil"/B-cell-derived granulocyte cluster appears specifically in ARDS patients; expresses C/EBP-related markers suggesting transdifferentiation, possibly driven by lung-derived signals (e.g. IL-24).
- Neutrophil counts in PBMCs not significantly elevated (consistent with PBMC purification depleting most neutrophils).

## Relevance to Trinetravir
**Critical platform mismatch**: this is the only one of our 4 PBMC studies that uses **Seq-Well**, not 10x Chromium. Seq-Well has very different gene-detection sensitivity (~3,200 genes/cell vs ~1,900–6,900 for 10x studies here) and 3' bias profile, making it the strongest expected source of cross-study technical batch effect in Phase 3 harmonization. Healthy controls **are** present but were collected from a *separate study* (different IRB protocol) — this is not a within-cohort matched control, so any "healthy-vs-COVID" contrast carries an irreducible site/protocol confound. Severity stratification is binary (ventilated/ARDS vs not), not WHO-ordinal — incompatible with finer-grained scales used by Lee/Schulte-Schrepping. Time-from-symptom-onset spans days 2–16 (acute phase only, no convalescent). All-male cohort with extreme age range — sex cannot be a covariate; age is.

## Caveats / limitations
- Authors' stated: very small cohort (n=7), single site, single sex, all severe enough to warrant hospitalization (no truly mild outpatient cases), most patients on remdesivir (treatment confound), short follow-up.
- For us additionally: Seq-Well chemistry is rare in cellxgene (this may be the only one in our 4-study set), so it will likely be the worst-integrated sample after Harmony/scVI. Healthy controls were from a separate study and likely a different time window. Patient C1 has two longitudinal samples — must decide whether to dedup or use both. ARDS-only "severe" tier means we cannot place Wilk patients on the same severity scale as Schulte-Schrepping (WHO ordinal 1-8) without manual recoding.
